Kerala rainfall: where do the products agree?

Three daily rainfall products (CHIRPS, IMD, ERA5-Land), 14 districts, 1981–2025. For each district: do the products agree on which years were extreme?

Agreement is not accuracy. There is no station ground truth here. A district is “agree” when the products move together year to year, not when any of them is right.
How to read this, and what it cannot tell you
  • Method: for each product, take every year’s maximum 7-day rainfall (or the June–September total) per district, then rank-correlate the 45 yearly values between each pair of products. 95% intervals come from resampling years.
  • Tiers (fixed in advance): agree = all three pairs have an interval entirely above 0; partial = at least one pair does, not all; none = no pair does.
  • “Agree” is a low bar. It means significantly positive, not strong. Read the correlation numbers.
  • Boundaries: CHIRPS and ERA5-Land use FAO GAUL 2015 (a deprecated version, with a visible Idukki/Ernakulam offset). IMD’s original series uses its own district outlines. Re-clipping IMD to the same GAUL polygons changed agreement by a median of 0.01 and moved no district to a different tier, so the outline choice does not explain these results (the largest shift was Idukki).
  • Small sample: 45 years, 14 districts × 3 pairs tested with no multiple-comparison correction. Districts near a tier boundary could flip.
  • Day alignment: IMD days end at 08:30 IST, so IMD day d+1 was matched to CHIRPS day d.
Products: CHIRPS v2 (UCSB-CHG), IMD gridded rainfall (India Meteorological Department), ERA5-Land (ECMWF), via Google Earth Engine and imdlib. Code and full findings in the project README.
